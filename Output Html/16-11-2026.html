Pradhan Mantri Janjatiya Vikas Mission

★ ★ ★ ☆ ☆

Source: PIB

Why in News?

The Prime Minister paid tribute to Bhagwan Birsa Munda on his 151st birth anniversary (15th November 2026), observed as Janjatiya Gaurav Diwas , highlighting his contribution to tribal rights, freedom and self-respect.

  • In this context, the Pradhan Mantri Janjatiya Vikas Mission (PMJVM) highlights efforts toward tribal economic empowerment through value addition, entrepreneurship and improved market access for tribal communities.

What are the Key Facts About Pradhan Mantri Janjatiya Vikas Mission?

  • About: Launched in 2021–22 , the Pradhan Mantri Janjatiya Vikas Mission (PMJVM) is a Central Sector Scheme aimed at improving tribal livelihoods through skill development, technology adoption, value addition, entrepreneurship and access to national and digital markets .
    • It seeks to transform tribal forest gatherers from raw produce sellers into community-based entrepreneurs , ensuring better returns from Minor Forest Produce (MFP).
  • Implementing Agency: The Tribal Cooperative Marketing Development Federation of India (TRIFED) , under the Ministry of Tribal Affairs, facilitates MFP procurement and marketing through State implementing agencies, including tribal cooperatives and forest development corporations.
  • Scale of Implementation: As of March 2026, 4,172 VDVKs had been sanctioned , covering approximately 12.48 lakh tribal members . By 31 July 2026, 2,911 VDVKs were functional , reporting sales of around ₹168 crore .

Van Dhan Vikas Kendras (VDVKs)

  • Community-Based Enterprises: PMJVM organises tribal MFP gatherers into Self-Help Groups (SHGs) , with 15 SHGs comprising approximately 300 members forming one VDVK.
    • At least 60% of VDVK members must belong to Scheduled Tribes.
  • Van Dhan Yojana : Launched on 14 April 2018 , it forms the foundation of the VDVK model and promotes tribal entrepreneurship through processing and value addition.
  • Skilling and Technological Support: VDVKs provide training in scientific and sustainable harvesting, processing and value addition , along with equipment such as cutters, sieves, decorticators, dryers and packaging tools.
    • Training support includes provision for 30 members , along with raw materials and trainee kits.
  • Financial and Infrastructure Support: VDVKs receive infrastructure support, working-capital facilitation, and storage and transportation assistance.
    • Working capital is facilitated through banks, financial institutions and the National Scheduled Tribes Finance and Development Corporation (NSTFDC) .
    • Processing infrastructure may be established in beneficiaries' homes or government/Gram Panchayat buildings, while storage and transportation support may be provided on a rental basis.

Marketing and Business Support

  • Market Linkages: VDVKs connect tribal producers with local and distant markets and are established around Haat Bazaars , which serve as major MFP procurement centres.
    • There are over 5,000 Haats in tribal areas , with phased modernisation involving permanent structures, storage, drinking water, shade and weighing facilities.
  • Business Partnerships: VDVKs may collaborate with private organisations and non-profits for value addition and marketing of tribal products.
  • Marketing and Branding: PMJVM supports branding, promotion, exhibitions, artisan melas, e-commerce platforms, geo-tagging, Geographical Indication (GI) registration and research and development of tribal products.
    • TRIFED provides market intelligence, including daily market rates and information on e-commerce opportunities, to improve business decisions.

How Are Tribal Livelihoods Protected and Empowered?

  • Importance of MFP: Minor Forest Produce such as honey, tendu leaves, mahua, tamarind, lac, bamboo and wild herbs contributes around 20–40% of annual income for many forest-dwelling tribal families.
    • Tribal gatherers often receive only a small share of the final market value due to dependence on intermediaries and distress sales.
  • Minimum Support Price (MSP) Mechanism: The Government has notified MSP for 87 MFP items , providing price protection to tribal gatherers through procurement at predetermined support prices.
    • Procurement Performance: Since 2013–14 , States have procured 2,67,954 metric tonnes of MFP worth ₹693.98 crore under the MSP procurement mechanism, which predates PMJVM.
  • Forest Rights Act (FRA), 2006 : Recognises the rights of forest-dwelling Scheduled Tribes and other traditional forest dwellers to collect, use and dispose of MFP.
    • It defines MFP as non-timber forest produce of plant origin, including bamboo, honey, wax, lac, tendu leaves, medicinal plants, roots and tubers.
  • PESA Act, 1996 : Provides for ownership of MFP by Gram Sabhas and Panchayats at the appropriate level in Scheduled Areas.

Tribal Women Empowerment

  • Women-Led Enterprises: Since women undertake a major share of MFP collection and sales, VDVKs promote women's financial empowerment, entrepreneurship and community ownership .
  • Example: The Nyigamane, Ho Yorbe, Medumhagdum VDVK in Leparada district, Arunachal Pradesh, established in October 2021, comprises 300 women SHG members and farmers .
    • It processes local produce into King Chilli pickle, bamboo shoot pickle, turmeric and ginger powder, pineapple jam and squash, and orange squash .
    • The enterprise has recorded approximately ₹11 lakh in sales since 2022–23 , helping women diversify their livelihoods beyond seasonal agriculture and become joint owners and stakeholders in the enterprise.

Janjatiya Gaurav Diwas

  • About: Observed annually on 15th November since 2021, Janjatiya Gaurav Diwas commemorates the birth anniversary of Dharti Aaba Bhagwan Birsa Munda and honours the contributions of tribal freedom fighters to India's independence and nation-building.
  • Janjatiya Gaurav Pakhwada 2026: Organised by the Ministry of Tribal Affairs from 1–15 November 2026 , it aims to accelerate flagship tribal welfare programmes and ensure last-mile delivery of benefits.
  • Development Projects: Over 300 tribal development projects were planned, including 228 for inauguration and 74 for foundation stone laying .
  • Digital Initiatives: The Jan-HIT Portal and National Forest Rights Act (FRA) Digital Platform were announced for launch to strengthen tribal health information systems and digital implementation of forest rights.
  • Tribal Research Initiatives: Tribal Research Institutes (TRIs) planned 160+ activities across 19 States/UTs to promote tribal culture, research and awareness.

Conclusion

The Pradhan Mantri Janjatiya Vikas Mission (PMJVM) strengthens tribal livelihoods through MSP-based protection, value addition, entrepreneurship and improved market access . Addressing implementation gaps, ensuring sustainable harvesting and promoting women-led enterprises will be crucial for achieving inclusive tribal development and a resilient forest-based economy .

Challenges to the Quad’s Strategic Cohesion

★ ★ ★ ☆ ☆

For Prelims: Quadrilateral Security Dialogue , NATO , Indian Ocean Region , Belt and Road Initiative , RCEP , Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Across Regions

For Mains: India's bilateral, regional and global groupings and agreements, Quad and the emerging Indo-Pacific security architecture, India’s strategic autonomy in minilateral groupings, Quad as a counter to China’s maritime and economic influence

Source: TH

Why in News?

Leaders of the Quadrilateral Security Dialogue (Quad) are scheduled to convene in Manila in November 2026 on the sidelines of the 49 th ASEAN Summit. This follows the 11 th Quad Foreign Ministers' Meeting held in New Delhi in May 2026, occurring against the backdrop of questions regarding the grouping's long-term cohesion and institutional momentum.

Summary

  • The Quad has evolved into a key Indo-Pacific strategic platform , enabling India to strengthen maritime security, counter China's growing influence and advance its strategic autonomy without entering a formal military alliance.
  • However, divergent geopolitical interests, institutional weaknesses, economic dependence on China and US policy uncertainty challenge its cohesion. Its future effectiveness depends on delivering tangible regional public goods while preserving ASEAN Centrality and an inclusive Indo-Pacific order.

What are the Major Challenges to the Quad’s Strategic Cohesion?

  • Institutional Vulnerability: Unlike NATO , the Quad lacks a permanent secretariat, a formal founding charter, or a mutual defense treaty.
    • Its momentum is entirely dependent on the political will of the incumbent leadership. As four democracies, the grouping is highly vulnerable to domestic electoral cycles, coalition dynamics , and shifting administrative priorities.
    • However, the Quad has demonstrated operational resilience despite political uncertainty. Although no Leaders' Summit was held in 2025, foreign ministers continued meeting, and cooperation advanced through initiatives such as the Indo-Pacific Maritime Surveillance Collaboration (IPMSC), launched in May 2026.
      • This suggests that the grouping's functional continuity is becoming less dependent on leader-level summits.
  • The Economic Architecture Vacuum: The Quad struggles to offer Southeast Asia a viable geo-economic alternative to China's Belt and Road Initiative (BRI) or regional dominance.
    • There is no Quad Free Trade Agreement (FTA) . With the US and India remaining outside mega-regional trade agreements like the CPTPP (Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership) and RCEP , the Quad’s economic deterrence is fundamentally weak.
    • Unilateral tariff actions, protectionist trade policies, and supply-chain bottlenecks among the four member nations (particularly between the U.S. and its partners) created significant friction in 2025, threatening to dilute the group's broader geopolitical alignment.
  • "Mission Creep" and "Dilution of Core Focus: In a bid to shed the "Asian NATO" label and appear non-confrontational, the Quad has rapidly expanded its mandate into non-traditional security domains (climate change, vaccines, STEM fellowships, space).
    • While some analysts warn that excessive diversification could dilute the Quad's strategic focus, cooperation in critical technologies, resilient supply chains, maritime surveillance and humanitarian assistance also strengthens its regional legitimacy.
    • The challenge is to balance traditional security objectives with the delivery of tangible regional public goods .
  • Uncertainty Over US Strategic Bandwidth: The Quad's long-term effectiveness depends heavily on the continuity of the US Pivot to Asia .
    • However, competing geopolitical priorities, including the Russia-Ukraine War, West Asian conflicts, and its emphasis on greater defence burden-sharing raise concerns about US strategic bandwidth and the reliability of its Indo-Pacific security commitments .
  • The Rise of "Hedging" and Minilaterals: Perceived unpredictability in Washington has prompted Indo-Pacific powers to diversify their security partnerships outside the core Quad framework:
    • Japan’s OCEAN Framework: Through its One Cooperative Effort Among Nations (OCEAN) , Japan is rapidly reshaping its post-war security posture and deepening strategic alignment directly with partner countries.
    • The JAI Trilateral: The Japan-Australia-India (JAI) Defence Ministers’ meeting in Cebu (Philippines) signals a willingness among regional middle powers to deepen hard-security cooperation independent of Washington’s regional role.
    • Extra-Regional Actors: European powers, notably France, are actively strengthening ties with regional countries to sustain a balanced security architecture.
    • India’s Multi-Alignment: India continues to aggressively diversify its defense ties with like-minded states across the Indian Ocean Region and Southeast Asia.

India’s Specific Challenges & Dilemmas Within the Quad

  • The Maritime vs. Continental Dilemma: The Quad is inherently a maritime grouping focused on the East China Sea, Taiwan Strait, and South China Sea.
    • However, India’s most immediate and active military threats are land-based (the Line of Actual Control with China and the border with Pakistan ).
    • The Quad offers no direct hard-security deterrence for India’s Himalayan borders , creating a severe mismatch in burden-sharing.
  • Balancing Russia in a Western Minilateral: Russia views the Quad as an exclusive, US-led mechanism.
    • India must walk a delicate diplomatic tightrope to ensure its active participation in the Quad does not alienate Moscow, which remains a crucial historical defense supplier (e.g., S-400 systems) and a vital source of discounted energy.
    • This heavily contrasts with the sanctions-driven approach of the other three members.
  • Reconciling FOIP and MAHASAGAR Frameworks: While the US emphasises a Free and Open Indo-Pacific (FOIP), India advocates an inclusive and multipolar regional order through its MAHASAGAR (Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Across Regions) framework.
    • Differences in strategic priorities and approaches towards China create challenges in maintaining policy convergence within the Quad.
  • Preserving the "Global South" Identity: India has meticulously positioned itself as the leader and voice of the Global South .
    • Deepening absolute strategic and military alignment with three advanced, Western-aligned economies risks diluting India's non-aligned credibility among developing nations, who often view groupings like the Quad as tools of Western bloc politics.
  • Divergence on Digital and Tech Governance: Within the Quad's Critical and Emerging Technology working groups, India frequently clashes with Japan and the US over global data governance.
    • While the US and Japan aggressively push the "Data Free Flow with Trust" (DFFT) model, India staunchly champions data localization, digital sovereignty, and digital protectionism.

Quad

  • About: The Quad is an informal strategic and diplomatic partnership between four democratic nations: India, the United States, Japan, and Australia .
  • Primary Objective: Its core mission is to ensure a "free, open, inclusive, and prosperous" Indo-Pacific region that is anchored by democratic values, the rule of law, freedom of navigation, and freedom from coercion.
  • Origins and Evolution: The grouping initially came together as a coordinated disaster-relief core group following the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami .
    • It was formally proposed as a strategic dialogue in 2007 by former Japanese Prime Minister Shinzo Abe , revived in 2017 (often called Quad 2.0) in response to China's growing military and economic assertiveness, and elevated to the head-of-state summit level in 2021.
  • Nature of the Partnership: Unlike NATO, the Quad is not a formal military alliance and does not have a mutual defense treaty, permanent secretariat, or founding charter. It operates as a flexible, issue-based coalition.
  • Core Focus Areas: The Quad pools its resources to provide regional public goods, focusing on:
    • Maritime Security: Enhancing maritime domain awareness (tracking illegal fishing and "dark ships") and conducting joint naval drills like the Malabar Exercise.
    • Economic and Technological Resilience: Securing critical mineral supply chains, deploying trusted telecommunications (Open RAN), and funding emerging technologies like Artificial Intelligence.
    • Humanitarian Assistance: Coordinating disaster relief, climate change mitigation efforts, and public health emergency responses across the Indo-Pacific and Southeast Asia.
  • India's Strategic Calculus in the Quad:
    • Strategic Autonomy: India uses the Quad to balance China's rise without formally entering a military alliance, preserving its traditional policy of strategic autonomy.
    • Net Security Provider: The Quad enhances India’s capabilities in maritime domain awareness (e.g., the Indo-Pacific Partnership for Maritime Domain Awareness - IPMDA ) and strengthens its role as a net security provider in the Indian Ocean Region (IOR).
    • Supply Chain Resilience : Quad complements the global China+1 strategy, creating opportunities for India to emerge as a key manufacturing and supply-chain hub.

Recent Strategic Milestones of the Quad (2025–2026)

  • Maritime Security: Launched the Indo-Pacific Maritime Surveillance Collaboration (IPMSC) in May 2026 to strengthen maritime domain awareness and track illegal dark ships through satellite, aerial and surface surveillance.
  • ASEAN Centrality: Reaffirmed commitment to the ASEAN Outlook on the Indo-Pacific (AOIP) in July 2026 to strengthen regional cooperation and address concerns over ASEAN's marginalisation.
  • Quad Critical Minerals Initiative: A strategic geopolitical framework designed to diversify rare earth supply chains , mobilize investments in mineral processing and recycling, and significantly reduce the Indo-Pacific's strategic economic dependence on China.
  • Digital Infrastructure & Tech Resilience: To secure regional communications against cyber threats, the Quad actively funds undersea cable connectivity for Pacific Island nations and champions the deployment of trusted, secure Open RAN telecommunications standards .
  • AI and Agriculture: Launched the AI-ENGAGE Initiative , providing over USD 6 million for AI and robotics-based agricultural research.
  • Disaster Preparedness: Pledged over USD 50 million to strengthen public health capacity and disaster response across the Indo-Pacific.

What Measures are Needed to Strengthen Quad?

  • Create a Strategic Secretariat: While a formal military treaty is unfeasible, establishing a minimal institutional structure (such as working-level secretariats for specific domains like critical minerals or cyber security) will provide continuity regardless of domestic political shifts.
  • Deliver Tangible Public Goods: The Quad must transition from joint strategic statements to on-the-ground execution . Providing high-quality infrastructure, telecommunications standards (like Open RAN), and climate tech funding is necessary to offer viable economic alternatives to China's Belt and Road Initiative (BRI).
  • Reinforce ASEAN Centrality: The Quad must consistently align its projects with the ASEAN Outlook on the Indo-Pacific (AOIP) to ensure organic buy-in and alleviate fears among Southeast Asian states that they are being sidelined.
  • Harmonize AUKUS and Quad: The US and Australia must ensure that AUKUS (a hard-power military pact) and the Quad (a comprehensive strategic dialogue) are viewed as complementary rather than competing architectures.
  • Focus on the Indian Ocean Region (IOR): To keep India deeply invested, the Quad must dedicate more operational bandwidth to the IOR, countering China's "String of Pearls" strategy through joint capability building in East Africa and island nations.
  • Deepen the "Quad Plus" Framework: Engaging strategically aligned nations like South Korea, Vietnam, and France on specific issues (like supply chain resilience) will organically expand the Quad's footprint without complicating its core decision-making loop.

Conclusion

The Quad's effectiveness lies in balancing strategic convergence with national autonomy . By strengthening maritime security, resilient supply chains and regional public goods while respecting ASEAN centrality, the grouping can contribute to a free, open, inclusive and rules-based Indo-Pacific .

Kerala to Revive Traditional Surangam Water-Harvesting System

★ ★ ☆ ☆ ☆

Source: TH

Eight grama panchayats in Kerala's Kasaragod district will undertake a project to restore traditional underground water-harvesting tunnels known as Surangams , aiming to improve groundwater recharge, conserve rainwater, reduce summer water shortages and sustain river flows amid declining rainfall and increasing drought risks.

  • Implementation: The project will utilize workers under the Viksit Bharat–Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin) (VB-G RAM G), which replaced MGNREGA on 1st July 2026.
  • Technical Support: The Central Ground Water Board (CGWB) will provide scientific guidance on tunnel restoration, groundwater recharge and sustainable water management.

Surangam Water-Harvesting System

  • Traditional Technique: Surangams are horizontal tunnels excavated into laterite hills to collect groundwater through natural seepage and gravity-driven flow.
  • Geographical Distribution: Primarily associated with Kasaragod district of Kerala and parts of Dakshina Kannada district in Karnataka, particularly in laterite-rich, undulating terrain.
  • Local Names: Surangams are also known as Thurangam, Thorapu and Mala in different localities. The term Surangam is derived from the Kannada word for tunnel.
  • Physical Dimensions: A typical Surangam is approximately 0.45–0.70 metres wide, 1.8–2 metres high and 3–300 metres long. Longer tunnels may include vertical ventilation shafts.
  • Water Collection: Groundwater emerging from the tunnels is generally collected in an open pit, pond or storage structure, sometimes called a Mathakkam , before being used for drinking water, domestic purposes or irrigation.
  • Significance: Provides a low-energy, gravity-based water supply system that supports local water security and demonstrates the value of indigenous water-management knowledge.

Exercise Sea Vigil 2026

★ ★ ☆ ☆ ☆

Source: PIB

The Indian Navy conducted the 5th edition of the biennial nationwide coastal defence exercise ‘Sea Vigil 2026’ covering India's entire coastline and Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) to assess maritime security preparedness and inter-agency coordination.

  • Background & Origin: The Sea Vigil exercise was conceptualized in 2018 to test the coastal security measures implemented after the 26/11 Mumbai terror attacks .
  • Strategic Importance: 'Sea Vigil' acts as a crucial precursor and build-up to the Indian Navy’s major biennial Theatre Level Readiness Operational Exercise (TROPEX) , which evaluates broader naval preparedness, including high-seas operations.
  • Multi-Agency Synergy: It is led by the Indian Navy and features a whole-of-government approach, integrating stakeholders like the Indian Coast Guard , Coastal Security Police, Customs, Intelligence Bureau (IB), and the Central Industrial Security Force (CISF).
  • Primary Objective: Activate the entire coastal defence mechanism across the nation, test technical information-sharing, and plug existing security gaps to secure India's maritime borders.
  • Two-Phased Execution:
    • Phase 1 - CDSRE (Coastal Defence and Security Readiness Evaluation): Involves Joint Audit Teams inspecting the security and operational preparedness of critical maritime infrastructure like major/minor ports, fish landing centres, and Vulnerable Areas and Vulnerable Points (VAs/VPs) .
    • Phase 2 - Tactical Phase: Involves live deployment of maritime assets dividing forces into a simulated 'Red Force' (attacking) and 'Blue Force' (defending) to evaluate real-time response mechanisms.
  • Contemporary Threat Mitigation: The exercise tests readiness against modern asymmetric threats, including drone attacks, merchant vessel hijackings , and covert strikes on vital coastal installations.

Retail Inflation Breaches RBI's Upper Tolerance Limit of 6%

★ ★ ☆ ☆ ☆

Source: TH

India’s headline retail inflation surged to 6.09% in October 2026 , breaching the Reserve Bank of India’s (RBI) upper tolerance threshold of 6% for the first time in two years, primarily driven by a sharp spike in food and fuel prices.

  • Current Inflation Data: The Consumer Price Index (CPI) based retail inflation rose to 6.09%, with food inflation jumping to 8.94% .
    • Notably, rural inflation (6.63%) outpaced urban inflation (5.41%) , indicating a higher cost-of-living burden on rural households.
  • Key Drivers of Inflation: The surge is heavily attributed to entrenched food inflation .
    • External shocks, such as 2026 West Asian geopolitical tensions raising fuel costs and a depreciating rupee making imports costlier, also significantly contributed.
  • Revised CPI Basket: This data reflects the newly implemented CPI base year 2024=100 series .
    • In this revised basket, Food and Beverages hold the highest weightage (~36.75%) , followed by Housing (~17.66%) , making the index highly sensitive to agricultural supply shocks.
  • RBI's Flexible Inflation Targeting (FIT): Instituted in 2016 following the recommendations of the Urjit Patel Committee , the RBI is legally bound under Section 45ZA of the RBI Act, 1934 to maintain price stability while keeping in mind the objective of growth.
    • The RBI is mandated to maintain a nominal anchor of 4% Consumer Price Index (CPI) inflation , governed by a tolerance band of +/- 2% (establishing an operating safe zone of 2% to 6%).
    • Reaffirming its commitment to macroeconomic stability, the Government of India has officially retained this 4% retail inflation target (with the same 2% to 6% tolerance band) for the third consecutive five-year cycle, effective from 1 st April 2026, to 31 st March 2031.
  • Statutory Accountability: Under Section 45ZN of the RBI Act , if inflation stays outside the 2–6% tolerance band for three consecutive quarters , the RBI is legally required to submit a report to the Central Government explaining the reasons, remedial actions, and an estimated time frame for returning to the target.
  • Monetary Policy Action: To combat these inflationary pressures, the six-member Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) raised the benchmark repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50% in October 2026, marking a shift toward "calibrated tightening" to manage demand and anchor inflation expectations.
