Balancing Economic Growth and Air Quality in India

★ ★ ★ ☆ ☆

For Prelims: Environmental Performance Index , International Energy Agency , Flue Gas Desulfurization , PM2.5 , Pradhan Mantri Ujjwala Yojana , National Clean Air Programme (NCAP)

For Mains: Sustainable development and balancing economic growth with environmental protection, Environmental governance

Source: TH

Why in News?

The debate over balancing economic growth with environmental protection has gained attention following India's poor performance in the 2026 Environmental Performance Index (EPI) , where it ranked 176 th out of 177 countries and 175 th in air quality .

  • A study published in Nature Sustainability found that aerosol pollution reduced India's solar power generation by 9.6% in 2023 , while the State of Global Air 2025 report estimated that more than 20 lakh deaths in India in 2023 could be attributed to air pollution.

Summary

  • India must balance economic development with air quality improvement , as pollution damages public health, labour productivity, energy generation and long-term economic growth.
  • The way forward lies in clean technology, airshed-based governance, stronger emission standards, affordable clean energy and outcome-based pollution control to achieve sustainable development and Viksit Bharat @2047.

What are the Challenges in Balancing Economic Growth with Air Quality for India?

  • Economic Growth with Air Quality Conflict: The core activities that drive India’s development industries, coal-fired power plants, freight transport, and agriculture are also the primary sources of emissions.
    • Upgrading to clean technology requires massive capital, which many fear could slow industrial output and economic growth.
  • Energy-Emission Paradox: According to the International Energy Agency (IEA) , India’s electricity demand will grow by an average of 6.4% annually through 2030 . While renewable solar energy is projected to meet half this demand, coal will still account for at least 25% , sustaining a high baseline of industrial emissions.
  • Policy Dilution for Cost-Effectiveness: Implementing advanced pollution control technology (like Flue Gas Desulfurization ) requires immense capital.
    • In July 2025, the Union government relaxed mandatory Sulphur Dioxide (SO 2 ) emission norms for several coal-fired power plants. The rationale was to control electricity prices and water consumption.
    • However, SO 2 is a primary precursor to the highly toxic PM2.5 (fine particulate matter), illustrating the tough trade-offs policymakers face.
  • "Sustained Use" Behavioral Challenge: Providing clean technology does not guarantee its use.
    • Under the Pradhan Mantri Ujjwala Yojana , millions gained LPG access.
    • However, high cylinder refill costs relative to household income often force the rural poor to revert to burning biomass, perpetuating lethal indoor and ambient air pollution.
  • Fragmented Governance vs. The Airshed: Air pollution is a transboundary issue. India’s current administrative approach fights pollution city-by-city (e.g., Delhi vs. Haryana), ignoring the Airshed approach required to tackle meteorological zones like the Indo-Gangetic Plain as a single ecological unit.
    • The city-by-city approach creates a collective-action problem where one city suffers from the pollution generated by a neighboring state (e.g., stubble burning) .
  • Economic Cost of Pollution: The perceived trade-off between economic growth and pollution control is misleading, as pollution itself causes significant economic losses.
    • Smog reduced India's solar power generation by 9.6% in 2023 by blocking sunlight.
    • According to a Dalberg Advisors–Clean Air Fund assessment , air pollution cost India an estimated USD 260 billion (around 6% of GDP) in 2024 , partly due to reduced worker productivity, healthcare costs and premature deaths. Presenteeism , where employees work while ill with reduced productivity, is a major contributor.
  • Climate Masking Paradox: Aerosols (pollution particles) scatter solar radiation and exert a cooling effect. A sudden removal of aerosols without reducing greenhouse gases could unmask this cooling, leading to a sudden spike in extreme heat.
  • Over-focus on PM10 over PM2.5: While cities have shown success in reducing PM10 (dust), PM2.5 (fine, highly toxic particles that often form secondarily from industrial gases like SO2 and NOx) remains dangerously high.
    • Falling PM10 does not guarantee a falling PM2.5 trend.

Air Quality in India

  • IQAir's World Air Quality Report 2025 : According to IQAir's World Air Quality Report 2025 , India's population-weighted annual average PM2.5 concentration declined by 3% , from 50.6 µg/m³ in 2024 to 48.9 µg/m³ in 2025 , improving its global ranking from 5th to 6th most polluted country .
    • However, New Delhi remained the world's most polluted capital , with an annual PM2.5 concentration of 82.2 µg/m³ , while Loni (Uttar Pradesh) emerged as India's most polluted city at 112.5 µg/m³ .
    • The report also highlighted weak enforcement of vehicular and industrial emission norms and inadequate allocation of National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) funds towards industrial pollution control.
  • CREA Report on India's Air Quality (October 2026): According to the Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air (CREA) , 261 out of 267 Indian cities exceeded the WHO's daily PM2.5 guideline of 15 µg/m³ in October 2026, while 38 cities breached India's national standard of 60 µg/m³ .
  • Environmental Performance Index (EPI) 2026: India ranked 176th out of 177 countries, with a rank of 175th in Air Quality but 88 th in Air Pollution, highlighting the gap between emissions-related performance and health outcomes.
  • NCAP Progress: In FY 2025–26, 108 of 130 NCAP cities recorded lower annual PM10 concentrations than in 2017–18, including 26 cities with reductions exceeding 40%.

What Measures are Needed to Balance Economic Growth with Air Quality?

  • Fostering Co-Benefits in Development: A Dalberg–Clean Air Fund assessment estimates that a 20% reduction in India's PM2.5 pollution could avert USD 85 billion in annual economic losses by 2030, highlighting the economic benefits of effective pollution control.
    • Therefore, pollution-control measures should prioritise interventions that address multiple socio-economic and environmental challenges simultaneously.
    • Industrial Upgrades: Enforcing the PAT (Perform, Achieve and Trade) Scheme and mandating Flue-Gas Desulphurisation (FGD) technology in thermal plants to cut toxic emissions while boosting energy efficiency.
    • Clean Cooking Transition: Strengthening the PMUY with targeted, income-based LPG refill subsidies to curb lethal indoor smoke and protect maternal health.
    • Scientific Waste Management: Leveraging Swachh Bharat Mission-Urban 2.0 and the GOBARdhan Scheme to eliminate open garbage burning through biomethanation.
  • Adopting Regional Airshed Governance: Establish a regional "airshed" management system, especially across the highly polluted Indo-Gangetic Plain.
    • Regional Airshed will ensure coordinated action against stubble burning and industrial emissions across state lines.
  • Transitioning to Outcome-Linked Funding: Financial allocations by the 16th Finance Commission and the MoEFCC (Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change) must be granted to reward verifiable, on-ground reductions in PM2.5 rather than mere expenditure on smog towers or sweeping machines.
  • Integrated Heat and Health Action Plans (HHAPs): Aligning with NDMA guidelines to build localized resilience against the "climate masking" effect, preparing vulnerable populations for the temporary localized warming that occurs when cooling aerosol pollutants are removed from the atmosphere.
  • The Chinese Precedent: Between 2005 and 2010 , while its economy expanded rapidly, China achieved a 14% drop in SO2 emissions .
    • China accounted for 54.9% of aerosol-related solar power losses worldwide in 2023. However, by strictly retrofitting its coal plants with high-efficiency filters, it managed to reduce its pollution-related solar power loss by 1.4% every year from 2013 to 2023, even as coal power expanded.
    • Researchers attributed this directly to strict emission controls rather than just favorable weather patterns.

Conclusion

Under Article 21 of the Indian Constitution , the Right to Life inherently includes the right to a pollution-free environment. By embracing targeted emission controls, outcome-based funding, and clean technology integration, India can fulfil its commitments to the Sustainable Development Goals (SDG 3, 7, 8, and 13) . The ultimate metric of a Viksit Bharat will not just be the size of its economy, but its ability to ensure that pollution falls significantly faster than its polluting activities grow.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. What is the National Clean Air Programme (NCAP)?
Launched in 2019 , NCAP is India's mission-mode programme to improve air quality across 131 non-attainment cities , targeting up to a 40% reduction in particulate matter concentrations or achievement of NAAQS.

2. What is an airshed-based approach to pollution control?
It treats an entire geographical region sharing common atmospheric conditions as one pollution-management unit, enabling coordinated action across cities and state boundaries .

3. What is the Graded Response Action Plan (GRAP)?
GRAP is an emergency pollution-control framework for Delhi-NCR that introduces progressively stricter measures based on AQI levels, including construction restrictions and curbs on certain vehicles.

4. What is the climate masking effect of aerosols?
Aerosols scatter and reflect incoming solar radiation, producing a temporary cooling effect. Their removal without simultaneous greenhouse gas reductions may reveal additional warming.

5. How does Article 21 relate to air pollution?
The Supreme Court has interpreted Article 21 (Right to Life) to include the right to a clean and healthy environment, making environmental protection an important constitutional obligation.

  • Centre of Excellence in Traditional Medicine: India announced its commitment to establish the BIMSTEC Centre of Excellence in Traditional Medicine at the Institute of Teaching and Research in Ayurveda (ITRA), Jamnagar, Gujarat , to promote collaborative research, capacity building, knowledge exchange and evidence-based traditional medicine practices.
  • India–Sri Lanka Ambulance Cooperation: On the sidelines of the meeting, an MoU was exchanged to supply 100 additional fully equipped ambulances to Sri Lanka's 1990 Suwa Seriya Emergency Ambulance Service , with Indian grant assistance of approximately USD 5.28 million .
  • Health ATMs for Sri Lanka: India announced support for 50 Medical Kiosks (Health ATMs) with grant assistance of up to SLR 225 million .
    • These will provide point-of-care diagnostics and telemedicine services at Sri Lanka's Arogya Primary Health Centres.
    • The initiative forms part of India's USD 450 million rehabilitation package following Cyclone Ditwah (2025) .

Nutrient-Based Subsidy (NBS) Scheme: Rabi 2026–27

★ ★ ★ ☆ ☆

Source: PIB

Why in News?

The Union Government approved the Nutrient-Based Subsidy (NBS) rates for Phosphatic and Potassic (P&K) fertilisers for the Rabi season 2026-27 , aiming to ensure the availability of key fertilizers at affordable prices and promote balanced agricultural nutrient use.

What are the Key Highlights of the Nutrient-Based Subsidy (NBS) Rates for Rabi 2026-27?

  • Financial Outlay and Duration: The revised rates are applicable from 1 st October 2026 to 31 st March 2027 , with a tentative budgetary outlay of Rs 62,370 crore - a significant 64% increase over the previous Rabi season.
    • Subsidies are recalibrated for fundamental macronutrients ( Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus (P), Potassium (K), and Sulphur (S)) to align with global market trends and promote balanced soil health.
  • Key Interventions and Special Provisions:
    • Fixed DAP Price: To shield farmers from global price volatility, Di-Ammonium Phosphate (DAP) will remain available at a fixed price of Rs 1,350 per bag , with similar provisions extended to imported/domestic Triple Superphosphate (TSP) and Monoammonium Phosphate (MAP).
    • Promoting Alternatives: A freight subsidy for Single Superphosphate (SSP) continues, promoting it as a cheaper, indigenous alternative to DAP . Subsidy on Ammonium Sulphate (alternative to Urea) will also continue.
    • Boost to Domestic Production: A targeted Sulphur Package will reimburse domestic manufacturers 20% of their actual procurement cost (subject to a floor price) for Sulphur and Sulphuric Acid to reduce import dependence.

What is the Nutrient Based Subsidy (NBS) Scheme?

  • About: The NBS Scheme is a central sector agricultural policy administered by the Department of Fertilizers under the Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers.
    • Implemented since April 2010, the scheme aims to ensure that farmers have access to essential fertilizers at affordable prices while promoting the balanced use of soil nutrients.
  • Key Features of the Scheme:
    • Nutrient-Linked Subsidies: Under the NBS, the government does not subsidize the fertilizer product as a whole. Instead, it provides a fixed amount of subsidy per kilogram of the specific nutrients it contains namely Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus (P), Potash (K), and Sulphur (S) .
      • The subsidy rates are determined on an annual or bi-annual basis for the Kharif and Rabi agricultural seasons.
    • Fertilizer Coverage: The scheme currently covers 28 grades of Phosphatic and Potassic (P&K) fertilizers.
      • This includes widely used fertilizers like DAP, Muriate of Potash (MOP), and Single Super Phosphate (SSP).
    • Decontrolled Pricing: The P&K fertilizer sector operates under a decontrolled, market-driven regime.
      • Manufacturers and importers are permitted to fix the Maximum Retail Price (MRP) at reasonable levels based on global raw material costs . The government monitors these prices to prevent exploitation and ensure affordability.
    • Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT): The government disburses the fixed subsidy directly to the fertilizer companies, but only after the actual physical sale to a farmer is authenticated via Point-of-Sale (PoS) biometric/Aadhaar devices at retail outlets.
    • Crucial Exception (Urea): Urea is excluded from the NBS regime . It remains under a separate statutory price-controlled mechanism where the government strictly fixes its Maximum Retail Price.
    • Incentives for Fortification: The scheme provides additional financial incentives for fertilizers that are customized or fortified with secondary and micro-nutrients to promote crop-specific soil health.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. What is the Nutrient-Based Subsidy (NBS) Scheme?
Introduced in April 2010 , it provides fixed per-kilogram subsidies on the nutrients Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus (P), Potassium (K) and Sulphur (S) in P&K fertilisers.

2. Which fertilisers are covered under the NBS Scheme?
It covers 28 grades of Phosphatic and Potassic (P&K) fertilisers , including DAP, MOP and SSP.

3. Is urea covered under the NBS Scheme?
No. Urea is excluded and remains under a separate statutory price-control mechanism, with its MRP fixed by the government.

4. How does DBT operate under the NBS Scheme?
Subsidies are transferred directly to fertiliser companies after actual sales to farmers are authenticated through Point-of-Sale (PoS) devices using Aadhaar-based authentication .

One Nation One Ration Card (ONORC)

★ ★ ★ ☆ ☆

Source: PIB

Why in News?

The Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution highlighted the progress of the One Nation One Ration Card (ONORC) initiative in strengthening nationwide food security through ration portability, digital integration and improved Public Distribution System (PDS) delivery, particularly benefiting migrant workers and vulnerable households.

What are the Key Facts About One Nation One Ration Card?

  • About: Launched in 2019 by the Department of Food and Public Distribution, ONORC enables beneficiaries under the National Food Security Act (NFSA), 2013 to access their entitled foodgrains from any e-PoS-enabled Fair Price Shop (FPS) across India using their existing ration cards.
    • It supports both inter-State and intra-State portability, particularly benefiting migrant workers and their families.
    • Split Ration Entitlement: Migrant beneficiaries can collect their full or partial foodgrain entitlement at their destination, while family members remaining in their home State can collect the balance using the same ration card.
  • Nationwide Coverage: Implemented across all 36 States/Union Territories, covering 100% of Pradhan Mantri Garib Kalyan Anna Yojana (PMGKAY) beneficiaries.
  • Portability Transactions: Approximately 3.27 crore portability transactions are recorded every month. Since its inception, ONORC has facilitated over 233 crore transactions , distributing more than 614 lakh metric tonnes (LMT) of foodgrains.
  • Digitisation of PDS:
    • 100% of ration card and beneficiary databases have been digitised across States/UTs, covering approximately 78.65 crore beneficiaries as of October 2026.
    • Aadhaar Seeding: Of 20.51 crore ration cards, 98.8% (20.27 crore) are Aadhaar-seeded, while 76.97 crore beneficiaries have been linked with Aadhaar.
    • e-KYC: Completed for 71.52 crore beneficiaries (91%) as of September 2026.
  • e-PoS-enabled Fair Price Shops: Approximately 99.8% (5.50 lakh) of India's 5.51 lakh FPSs are operational with electronic Point of Sale (e-PoS) devices, supporting authenticated foodgrain distribution and portability.
  • Supply Chain Transparency: Online foodgrain allocation has been implemented nationwide, while 31 States/UTs have computerised their PDS supply chains. Online grievance redressal systems and transparency portals further strengthen accountability.
  • Rightful Targeting: Aadhaar-linked verification and PDS data analytics help identify duplicate and potentially ineligible beneficiaries.
    • Under Rightful Targeting Version 1 , approximately 5.95 crore suspicious records were flagged, resulting in the deletion of 2.58 crore duplicate or ineligible entries following verification.
  • SARTHAK-PDS: The Scheme for Assistance in Ration Transport and Handling–Income with Automation in PDS (SARTHAK-PDS) is an umbrella initiative supporting broader PDS reforms.
    • Approved for continuation until 31st March 2031 , with a Central financial outlay of ₹25,530 crore, it provides assistance for intra-State foodgrain transportation, handling and FPS dealers' margins while promoting technological modernisation.
    • NIRMAL: Strengthens PDS data architecture and analytics.
    • SAKSHAM: Enables real-time data integration and supply-chain monitoring, including Vehicle Location Tracking Systems (VLTS) .
    • SMART-PDS (Scheme for Modernisation and Reforms through Technology in Public Distribution System): Integrates ration card management, foodgrain distribution, supply chains and grievance redressal into a unified digital framework.

Mera Ration App

  • About: Developed under ONORC, the Mera Ration mobile application , available in 13 languages , facilitates access to ration-related information and services, particularly for migrant beneficiaries.
  • Key Features:
    • Check monthly foodgrain entitlements and transaction history.
    • Verify Aadhaar-seeding status and national portability eligibility.
    • Access voluntary ONORC registration services.
    • Locate nearby Fair Price Shops (FPSs) using GPS and map-based navigation.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. What is One Nation One Ration Card (ONORC), and when was it launched?
ONORC was launched in 2019 by the Department of Food and Public Distribution to enable NFSA beneficiaries to access foodgrains from any e-PoS-enabled Fair Price Shop across India using their existing ration cards.

2. What is the coverage of ONORC as of 2026?
ONORC covers all 36 States/UTs and 100% of PMGKAY beneficiaries, recording over 233 crore portability transactions and distributing more than 614 LMT of foodgrains.

3. What is SARTHAK-PDS?
SARTHAK-PDS is an umbrella scheme integrating financial support for foodgrain transportation and FPS dealer margins with technology-driven PDS reforms, including SMART-PDS, NIRMAL and SAKSHAM. It has been approved for continuation until March 2031.

4. What are the key features of the Mera Ration App?
Available in 13 languages, the app enables beneficiaries to check foodgrain entitlements, view transaction histories, verify Aadhaar-seeding status and locate nearby Fair Price Shops using GPS.

5. How does ONORC strengthen food security and welfare delivery?
ONORC promotes portability of welfare benefits, migrant inclusion, transparency and efficient last-mile delivery through Aadhaar authentication, digitised ration cards and nationwide PDS integration.

Nationwide Khet Bachao Abhiyan Launched

★ ★ ☆ ☆ ☆

Source: DD

The Union Minister for Agriculture and Farmers’ Welfare launched the nationwide Khet Bachao Abhiyan from Taraori in Karnal district, Haryana, to promote soil conservation, sustainable agriculture and prevention of agricultural land degradation.

  • About: The Khet Bachao Abhiyan was first launched on 1st June 2026 from Ramasiya village in Raisen district, Madhya Pradesh , as a nationwide awareness campaign to promote soil health, balanced fertiliser application, natural farming, water conservation and scientific agricultural practices.
  • Current Campaign Duration: The renewed nationwide awareness drive runs from 22nd October to 30th November 2026, focusing on preventing soil degradation and encouraging sustainable farming practices.
  • Three Key Measures for Soil Conservation:
    • Balanced Fertiliser Use: Encourage farmers to use Soil Health Cards to identify nutrient deficiencies and apply fertilisers in appropriate quantities based on soil and crop requirements, preventing excessive nutrient application and soil degradation.
    • Crop Rotation: Promote cultivation of pulses such as chickpea, lentil and pigeon pea instead of repeatedly following the paddy–wheat cycle, as pulses improve soil fertility through biological nitrogen fixation .
    • Natural Farming: Encourage farmers to initially adopt natural farming on at least half an acre , using preparations such as Beejamrit and Ghanjeevamrit to reduce dependence on chemical inputs and potentially lower cultivation costs.
  • Crop Residue Management: Discourage stubble burning , which causes air pollution and destroys beneficial soil microorganisms, insects and earthworms essential for maintaining soil fertility and organic matter decomposition.
  • Village-Level Outreach: Scientists from the Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) , agricultural universities and government departments will conduct village-level meetings and farmer awareness programmes to promote balanced nutrient management, crop rotation and sustainable farming practices.
  • Significance: The campaign aims to improve soil health, nutrient-use efficiency, agricultural productivity and environmental sustainability through scientific farming practices and active farmer participation.
