National Coastal Mission 2.0

★ ★ ★ ☆ ☆

Source: PIB

Why in News?

Recently, the Union Environment Minister launched the implementation Guidelines for National Coastal Mission 2.0 (NCM 2.0) in New Delhi.

What are the Key Facts About National Coastal Mission 2.0?

  • About: National Coastal Mission 2.0 (NCM 2.0) is a Central Sector Scheme with an approved outlay of ₹767 crore for 2025-26 to 2030-31, aligned with the Finance Commission award period.
    • It has been formulated as the consolidated framework for climate-resilient coastal management, ecosystem restoration and sustainable livelihoods, subsuming the earlier NCM from 16th May 2025.
  • Background: The Mission builds on India’s Blue Economy vision , earlier National Coastal Mission initiatives, the World Bank-assisted pilot project on Integrated Coastal Zone Management (ICZMP), and the C onservation and Management of Mangroves and Coral Reefs scheme.
  • Vision: It seeks to conserve and protect coastal and marine ecosystems while promoting inclusive development, climate resilience and sustainable utilisation of coastal resources to unlock India’s Blue Economy potential.
  • Objectives: Major objectives include climate adaptation and mitigation, conservation of mangroves, brackish-water wetlands and coral reefs, pollution control, sustainable livelihoods, capacity building, Integrated Coastal Zone Management Plans (ICZMPs) and Coastal Marine Spatial Plans (CMSPs).
  • Five Components: The Mission covers Climate Resilient Blue Economic Growth; Coastal Vulnerability and Risk Reduction; Climate Change Adaptation with mitigation co-benefits; Capacity Building and Outreach; and Committed Liabilities and Project Management.
  • Geographical Coverage: It covers nine coastal States—Gujarat, Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha and West Bengal—and four UTs—Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu, Andaman & Nicobar Islands, Puducherry and Lakshadweep.
  • Expected Outcomes: NCM 2.0 envisages ICZMPs and CMSPs for 1,000 km of priority coastline, cumulative vulnerability assessment of India’s coastline, restoration of coral reefs, mangrove restoration in the Andaman & Nicobar Islands, improved coastal water quality, green shoreline infrastructure, eco-friendly tourism and conservation of coastal mammals.
  • Significance: The Mission aims to reduce coastal vulnerability to erosion, flooding, and salinity ingress; strengthen institutional capacity and community livelihoods; and support sustainable Blue Economy -led development in line with Atmanirbhar Bharat, Blue Economy Vision 2047, and Viksit Bharat.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Q1. What is National Coastal Mission 2.0?
National Coastal Mission 2.0 is a Central Sector Scheme aimed at strengthening the resilience and sustainability of India’s coastal regions and supporting the Blue Economy.

Q2. What is the approved outlay of NCM 2.0?
The Mission has an approved outlay of ₹767 crore.

Q3. Till when will NCM 2.0 be implemented?
NCM 2.0 will be implemented till 2030–31.

Q4. Which areas are covered under NCM 2.0?
The Mission covers 9 coastal States and 4 Union Territories.

Q5. What are the major focus areas of NCM 2.0?
Its major focus areas include coastal vulnerability assessment, ecosystem restoration, pollution management, biodiversity conservation, sustainable tourism, spatial planning, research and capacity building.

India Attends SCO Meeting in Pakistan

★ ★ ☆ ☆ ☆

Source: IE

A senior Indian diplomat visited Pakistan to attend the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO) Council of National Coordinators meeting. This marks the first official visit by an Indian delegation to Pakistan since the recent military confrontations ( Operation Sindoor) .

  • Obligation as a Member State: After assuming the SCO presidency at the Bishkek Summit , Pakistan is hosting the organisation’s meetings during 2026–27 . As a full member of the SCO , India’s participation is both a diplomatic and institutional commitment .
    • Attending SCO meetings enables India to remain engaged in the organisation’s decision-making processes and maintain situational awareness.
  • About: The SCO is a 10-member Eurasian grouping comprising India, China, Russia, Pakistan, Iran, Belarus, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan and Uzbekistan, with Pakistan currently holding the rotating Chairmanship.
  • Pakistan’s SCO Chairmanship: Pakistan announced the theme “Turning Vision into Action: Connectivity, Innovation and Shared Prosperity” and will host the SCO Council of Heads of State Summit in August 2027.
  • Takeaways for India:
    • Diplomatic Balancing: India’s participation helps monitor Pakistan’s agenda and counter any misuse of the SCO platform on PoK and Kashmir .
    • Future India-Pakistan Engagement: The meeting can help assess the possibility of future high-level diplomatic engagement , including a potential Prime Ministerial visit to Pakistan.
    • Managing the China Challenge: Engagement through the SCO can help India maintain stability on its western front while focusing greater attention on the China border challenge .
