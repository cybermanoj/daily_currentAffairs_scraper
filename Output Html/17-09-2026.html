Kishau Multipurpose Project

★ ★ ★ ☆ ☆

Source: IE

In a major push to resolve long-pending inter-state water disputes, six northern states Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Haryana, Rajasthan, and Delhi signed a historic Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) to pave the way for the Kishau Multipurpose Project .

  • Background: A  MoU was signed in 1994 , between the Yamuna basin states (undivided Uttar Pradesh, Haryana, Rajasthan, Himachal Pradesh, and Delhi) regarding the allocation of the river's surface water.
    • Because the Upper Yamuna (from its source at the Yamunotri glacier to the Okhla barrage in Delhi) lacked major storage facilities, the 1994 agreement mandated the creation of three major storage projects on the Yamuna and its tributaries:
      • Lakhwar Project: On the Yamuna River (Uttarakhand).
      • Renukaji Project: On the Giri River (Himachal Pradesh).
      • Kishau Project: On the Tons River (Himachal Pradesh/Uttarakhand border).
  • Kishau Multipurpose Project: The Kishau Multipurpose Project entails the construction of a 232.6-metre-high concrete gravity dam on the Tons river (a major tributary of the Yamuna) situated along the Uttarakhand-Himachal Pradesh border.
  • Dispute: Designated as a National Project, the Centre funds 90% of the water component, with the remaining 10% shared among the basin states under the 1994 Yamuna framework.
    • However, the dispute centered on the power component, where Himachal Pradesh refused to bear its estimated Rs 2,000-crore liability , arguing that it was unfair to impose this fiscal burden on the state when it was already shouldering the displacement and ecological costs while downstream states received most of the water and irrigation benefits.
  • Breakthrough: A consensus was reached through "cooperative federalism." To relieve Himachal Pradesh’s financial constraints, the beneficiary downstream states agreed to cover its power-component liability.
    • According to the agreement, Delhi (biggest beneficiary of the project) and Rajasthan will bear this expense in a 75:25 ratio, as they are major beneficiaries of the water supply.
  • Socio-Economic Outcomes: The 422-MW project is designed to generate 1,476 million units of hydropower and will create vast irrigation potential covering 97,000 hectares of agricultural land.
  • Water Security for the Capital: Delhi stands to be the biggest beneficiary , receiving 110 million cubic metres (MCM) of water , which will provide critical, long-term support for the city's drinking water needs.
  • Environmental Rejuvenation: Correcting historical allocation flaws, 25% of the project's water is strictly dedicated to maintaining the environmental flow of the Yamuna river .
    • This is a crucial step toward the government's target of achieving a completely clean Yamuna by 2029 , which is linked to the National Mission for Clean Ganga (NMCG) .

Sanjukta Parasor: 1st Woman Officer to Lead CRPF’s CoBRA Unit

★ ★ ☆ ☆ ☆

Source: IE

Senior IPS officer Sanjukta Parasor has been appointed as the first woman Inspector General (IG) of the CRPF’s elite Commando Battalion for Resolute Action (CoBRA) unit , marking a significant milestone in India’s security forces. She will succeed 1996-batch IPS officer Danesh Rana.

  • About: A 2006-batch IPS officer, she is known as the “Iron Lady of Assam” for her counter-insurgency work in the Northeast, involving field policing, national security operations and handling of insurgency-related challenges.
    • She previously served as a Deputy Inspector General in the National Investigation Agency (NIA) , working on terrorism and extremism-related investigations.
  • Significance: Her appointment marks a milestone in women’s leadership in India’s security forces , particularly in specialised counter-insurgency and jungle warfare operations.

About CoBRA

  • The Commando Battalion for Resolute Action (CoBRA) is a specialised unit of the Central Reserve Police Force (CRPF) established to conduct jungle warfare and guerrilla operations , particularly against Maoist insurgency. Its personnel are selected from the CRPF and receive intensive training in commando tactics and jungle warfare.
  • Formation and Deployment: A total of 10 CoBRA units were raised between 2008 and 2011 . These battalions are deployed in Left Wing Extremism (LWE) -affected and insurgency-prone areas across states such as Chhattisgarh, Odisha, Jharkhand, Maharashtra, Assam and Meghalaya.
  • Role and Capabilities: CoBRA specialises in anti-Naxal operations, intelligence-based missions, long-range patrols, ambush and counter-ambush operations in challenging terrains, making it one of the CRPF’s key specialised operational forces.

National Conference on Quality and Safety of Biosimilars

★ ★ ☆ ☆ ☆

Source: PIB

Recently, the Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission (IPC) organised a two-day National conference on “Quality and Safety of Biosimilars: Strengthening India’s Biopharma SHAKTI Vision” in Bengaluru focusing on strengthening India’s biopharmaceutical ecosystem through quality, innovation and regulatory excellence.

  • The conference focused on improving the quality, safety, regulatory evaluation and development of biosimilars through collaboration among regulators, industry, academia and research organisations.
  • IPC released the Indian Pharmacopoeia Reference Substance (IPRS) for Enoxaparin Sodium for bioassays, providing an authenticated reference material for accurate and consistent testing of biological activity.
  • About IPC: IPC is an autonomous institution under the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare responsible for developing standards for medicines and strengthening pharmaceutical quality infrastructure.

Biosimilars

  • About: Biosimilars are highly similar versions of approved biological medicines that provide safe and effective treatment options for various conditions, including cancer, arthritis, kidney diseases, psoriasis, Crohn’s disease and colitis .
  • Benefits: They help improve patient access to essential medicines by offering additional treatment options and may reduce healthcare costs.
  • Biopharma SHAKTI Vision: It was announced in the Union Budget 2025-26 ,  with an outlay of ₹10,000 crore over five years to strengthen India’s ecosystem for biologics and biosimilars, promote domestic manufacturing and reduce import dependence.
  • Research and Clinical Infrastructure: The initiative focuses on expanding biopharma capabilities through the establishment of three new National Institutes of Pharmaceutical Education and Research (NIPERs) , upgradation of seven existing NIPERs , and creation of over 1,000 accredited clinical trial sites to support research, innovation and clinical development.
