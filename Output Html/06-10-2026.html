India’s 1st 5.56 km Free-Space Quantum Key Distribution Link Demonstrated

★ ★ ★ ☆ ☆

Source: PIB

Why in News?

India has successfully demonstrated its first 5.56 km Free-Space Quantum Key Distribution (QKD) link. The field trial established a secure quantum communication channel between BISAG-N (Bhaskaracharya National Institute for Space Applications and Geo-informatics) and IIT Gandhinagar . The demonstration was executed in collaboration with QNu Labs , an Indian deep-tech startup.

What is Free-Space Quantum Key Distribution (QKD)?

  • Quantum Key Distribution (QKD): It is a secure communication method that uses the quantum properties of photons to exchange cryptographic keys between two distant parties.
    • It does not transmit the actual message through quantum states . Instead, QKD securely establishes an encryption key, which is then used by conventional systems to encrypt and decrypt the actual data .
    • Free-Space QKD is a quantum-secure communication technology that transmits encryption keys through laser beams in open air , eliminating the need for physical fibre-optic cables.
    • QKD relies on Heisenberg’s Uncertainty Principle and the No-Cloning Theorem.
  • Heisenberg’s Uncertainty Principle: According to quantum mechanics, measuring a quantum system inherently alters it. If an eavesdropper tries to intercept the photons, the quantum state collapses, instantly alerting the communicating parties of a breach.
  • No-Cloning Theorem: It states that it is physically impossible to create a perfect, identical copy of an unknown quantum state, preventing hackers from secretly duplicating the transmitted keys.
  • Eavesdropper Detection: QKD does not transmit the actual message. Instead, it generates a secure cryptographic key used to encrypt the data.
    • Any attempt to intercept or measure the quantum states disturbs them and introduces detectable errors, allowing the users to detect eavesdropping and discard the compromised key.
  • Atmospheric Challenges: Unlike fibre-based QKD, free-space QKD requires direct line-of-sight between transmitter and receiver. Precise alignment is necessary, while atmospheric turbulence and ambient light pollution can distort or weaken the optical signal.

India's Breakthrough

The recent 5.56 km trial is a major leap from earlier short-distance tests (like the 300m test in 2021). Key highlights include:

  • Quantum Bit Error Rate (QBER): Measures the proportion of quantum bits received incorrectly. Lower QBER means a more reliable and secure quantum channel. In the 5.56 km trial, QBER remained below 5%.
  • Advanced Tracking: The trial successfully utilized QNu Labs’ precise Pointing, Acquisition, and Tracking (PAT) system to perfectly align the laser beam across open air over the entire distance.
  • Two-Layered Security Architecture: The demonstration combined QNu Labs' hardware ( Armos ) with BISAG-N’s software ( Vedic Kavach ).
    • This created a hybrid setup where QKD was layered with Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) (encryption algorithms designed to remain secure against quantum computers) .
    • If the physical atmospheric link drops temporarily due to weather, the mathematical PQC software continues to protect the data.
  • Significance for India:
    • Precursor to Satellite QKD: Free-space terrestrial QKD is a key stepping stone toward satellite-based quantum communication, as both rely on transmitting quantum signals through the atmosphere between line-of-sight optical terminals.
    • National Security: As quantum computers become advanced enough to break classical encryption algorithms, this technology provides a "quantum-safe" shield for defence, strategic infrastructure, and financial networks against future cyber threats.
    • National Quantum Mission Target: India’s National Quantum Mission (NQM) aims to develop satellite-based secure quantum communication between ground stations separated by up to 2,000 km, along with a 2,000-km inter-city QKD network using optical fibre.

Note : Earlier, under the NQM, startup QNu Labs demonstrated India’s first 500 km QKD network using existing optical-fibre infrastructure, strengthening quantum-secure communication and cyber defence.

  • On a global scale, China launched the world’s first quantum-communication satellite, Micius, in 2016. It operates in low-Earth orbit at around 500 km. China later launched Jinan-1 in 2022, which demonstrated real-time QKD with portable ground stations.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

1. What is Quantum Key Distribution (QKD)?
QKD is a secure method of exchanging cryptographic keys using quantum properties of photons , enabling detection of interception attempts.

2. What is Free-Space QKD?
Free-Space QKD transmits quantum encryption keys through laser beams in open air , without requiring physical fibre-optic cables.

3. What was significant about India’s 5.56 km QKD trial?
It demonstrated a 5.56 km secure free-space quantum communication link between BISAG-N and IIT Gandhinagar , supporting India’s future satellite-QKD capabilities.

4. Why is Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) important?
PQC uses quantum-resistant mathematical algorithms to protect conventional data from future attacks by powerful quantum computers.

5. What are the major challenges of Free-Space QKD?
Its deployment is affected by atmospheric turbulence, fog, rain and daylight , while requiring highly precise Line-of-Sight and Pointing, Acquisition and Tracking (PAT) systems.
