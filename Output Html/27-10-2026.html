Long-Term Strategic Vision and Land Warfare Doctrine

★ ★ ☆ ☆ ☆

Source: PIB

The Defence Minister addressed the Army Commanders' Conference in New Delhi, where he unveiled the Land Warfare Doctrine 2026 .

  • Need for a 20-30 Year Vision: Given the rapid shifts in global geopolitics and military technology, the Defence Minister stressed that ad-hoc planning must be replaced with a long-term strategic blueprint (20-30 years) to future-proof the armed forces.
    • Emphasizing that modern warfare is constantly evolving, noted that military strategies cannot be static. They must remain highly flexible and dynamic to enable timely adjustments to changing operational circumstances.
  • Year of Reforms: Referring to 2025 as the 'Year of Reforms,' the Defence Minister urged commanders to ensure that structural and administrative reforms translate into tangible operational capabilities on the ground.
  • Multi-Domain Operations (MDO): Emphasised jointness across military branches, the continued importance of ground forces, and the integration of emerging technologies such as Artificial Intelligence (AI), autonomous weapon systems (drones), data analytics, cybersecurity, and secure communication networks.
  • Aatmanirbharta in Defence (Self-Reliance): Celebrated massive milestones in indigenisation, noting that defence production reached a record Rs 1.78 lakh crore and defence exports hit an all-time high of Rs 38,000 crore in FY 2025-26.
  • Global Peacekeeping Footprint: Released the annual United Nations Journal and felicitated Indian Army personnel for their exemplary contributions to UN Peacekeeping missions and international security.

Land Warfare Doctrine 2026

  • About: The land warfare doctrine provides a strategic framework for preparing and conducting land-based operations, integrating unmanned systems, manned-unmanned teaming (MUM-T), and resilient logistics to address future conflicts.
  • Strategic Context: The doctrine updates the previous 2018 version, drawing crucial lessons from the prolonged standoff with China along the Line of Actual Control (LAC) , recent global conflicts ( Russia-Ukraine , Iran-US ), and Operation Sindoor against Pakistan.
  • Centrality of Land Power: Despite the rise of new warfare domains, the doctrine reiterates the continued centrality of land power in achieving decisive military and political outcomes.

India Achieves 11 Crore Farmer IDs Under Digital Agriculture Mission

★ ★ ☆ ☆ ☆

Source: PIB

The Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers’ Welfare achieved the target of creating 11 crore Farmer IDs under the Digital Agriculture Mission , five months ahead of the 31st March 2027 deadline.

  • Achievement: The target was completed on 23rd October 2026, with over 6 crore farmers already receiving benefits through Farmer ID-based service delivery.
  • About Farmer ID: A unique digital identity for farmers, created by linking farmers' details with their digitised land records under the Farmer Registry of AgriStack, facilitating access to agricultural services and government schemes.

Digital Agriculture Mission

  • About: Approved by the Union Cabinet on 2nd September 2024, under the Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers’ Welfare, with a total outlay of ₹2,817 crore, including a central share of ₹1,940 crore .
  • Objective: Develop Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) for agriculture to enable data-driven governance, efficient service delivery and farmer-centric digital solutions.
  • Key Components:
    • AgriStack: Includes the Farmer Registry, Geo-referenced Village Maps and Crop Sown Registry , created and maintained by States/UTs.
    • Krishi Decision Support System (Krishi DSS): Integrates satellite imagery, weather, soil, reservoir and groundwater data to support crop mapping, yield estimation, drought/flood monitoring and evidence-based agricultural planning .
    • Soil Profile Mapping: Supports village-level mapping of soil characteristics for sustainable land use and crop planning.

RBI’s Data Centre and Cybersecurity Training Institute

★ ★ ☆ ☆ ☆

Source: PIB

Recently, the Prime Minister inaugurated the Reserve Bank of India ’s newly established Data Centre and Cybersecurity Training Institute in Bhubaneswar, Odisha.

  • Key Features: The ₹169-crore project includes four state-of-the-art data centres, certified to the Uptime Institute's Tier IV standard, the highest tier for data-centre infrastructure resilience.
  • Critical Financial Infrastructure: The facility will support mission-critical payment and settlement systems, including NEFT and RTGS , along with RBI's e-Kuber platform for government banking operations, enhancing operational continuity and reducing service disruptions.
  • Digital Sovereignty: The RBI-operated facility will function independently of third-party cloud service providers, strengthening control over critical financial infrastructure.
  • Cybersecurity Training Institute: It will provide specialised training and research opportunities for RBI personnel, banks and other financial-sector institutions, focusing on Artificial Intelligence, Big Data Analytics , Quantum Computing and Blockchain .
  • Disaster Resilience: Bhubaneswar offers geographical separation from RBI's three existing data centres in Maharashtra and is situated in a different seismic zone, reducing the risk of simultaneous disruptions.
  • Significance: The facilities will strengthen financial-system resilience, cybersecurity preparedness, digital sovereignty and institutional capacity , supporting secure and reliable financial services.
