Khadi and Village Industries Commission (KVIC)

★ ★ ★ ☆ ☆

Source: PIB

Why in News?

The Ministry of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSME) highlighted the growing role of Khadi and Village Industries in transforming traditional skills and local resources into modern rural enterprises through access to finance, technology and training, with the Khadi and Village Industries Commission (KVIC) at the centre of this transformation.

  • The Khadi Mahotsav, organised annually from 2nd to 31st October, complements these efforts by promoting Khadi and local products, raising awareness among youth, and encouraging public participation in the Vocal for Local and Atmanirbhar Bharat initiatives.

What are the Key Facts About Khadi and Village Industries Commission (KVIC)?

  • About: The Khadi and Village Industries Commission (KVIC) i s a statutory body under the Ministry of Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises (MSME), established under the Khadi and Village Industries Commission Act, 1956.
  • Objective: To promote rural industrialisation, generate employment, strengthen village industries and support artisans through financial, technical and marketing assistance.

Initiatives for Market Promotion

  • International Market Linkages: KVIC facilitates participation in international exhibitions and trade fairs, helping rural enterprises connect with overseas buyers and expand their export opportunities.
  • Digital Market Access: KVIC operates the Khadi India online store , offering products such as clothing and fabrics, groceries, beauty products, essential oils, perfumes and incense sticks.
  • Retail Network: The Khadi marketing network includes 8 departmental Khadi India sales outlets, 15 KVIC branches and 8,035 sales outlets operated by Khadi Institutions across the country.
  • Global Khadi: Khadi is increasingly being presented through contemporary fashion platforms, including New Zealand Fashion Week 2026 and Lakmé Fashion Week 2025 , where traditional craftsmanship has been combined with modern designs.

Major Achievements of KVIC

  • Sectoral Growth: In 2025–26 , sales of Khadi and Village Industries products reached a record ₹1.87 lakh crore , while production increased by 380% to ₹1,25,296 crore compared with 2013–14.
  • Employment Generation: Employment in the sector increased by 56% to 2.04 crore between 2013–14 and 2025–26, highlighting the sector’s role in rural employment.
  • Training and Skill Development: KVIC operates 35 departmental and non-departmental training centres offering training in activities such as food processing, bakery, beekeeping, agarbatti and candle making, and motor repairing. During FY 2025–26, 79,682 individuals received training, including 47,382 women (approximately 59%).
  • Women’s Participation: Women constitute more than 80% of approximately five lakh artisans in the Khadi sector, highlighting its importance for women’s economic participation.

What are the Major Schemes Associated with KVIC?

  • PMEGP Employment: Since March 2008, the Prime Minister’s Employment Generation Programme (PMEGP) has provided employment opportunities to nearly 97.95 lakh people through KVIC .
  • Gramodyog Vikas Yojana: The Gramodyog Vikas Yojana (GVY) supports artisans by providing modern machines, toolkits and equipment, with 37,769 machines, toolkits and equipment distributed during 2025–26 and more than 3.23 lakh distributed by May 2026 .
  • Khadi Vikas Yojana: The scheme supports Khadi institutions through product improvement, value addition and market development. Its Modified Market Development Assistance (MMDA) component provides flexibility for value addition and market-oriented pricing. In 2024–25, the scheme disbursed ₹245 crore to 1,097 Khadi institutions, benefiting 1,45,484 artisans.
  • Concessional Credit: Under the Interest Subsidy Eligibility Certificate Scheme , eligible Khadi institutions can access credit at a concessional interest rate of 4% per annum .
  • Workshed Support: The Workshed Scheme provides improved production spaces and better working conditions for artisans.

Conclusion

The transformation of Khadi and Village Industries into market-oriented rural enterprises highlights the importance of integrating traditional skills with modern technology, financial support and market access. Strengthening this ecosystem can promote inclusive rural development, women’s economic empowerment, sustainable livelihoods and self-reliance in line with the vision of Atmanirbhar Bharat.

Starlink and Satellite Internet

★ ★ ★ ☆ ☆

Source: TH

Why in News?

A public dispute over Starlink’s proposed entry into India’s satellite internet market has raised concerns regarding market competition, regulatory approvals, national security and consumer affordability , highlighting the challenges surrounding the expansion of satellite-based internet services in India.

What are the Key Facts About Starlink?

  • About Starlink: Starlink is a satellite-based broadband internet service developed by SpaceX. It uses a constellation of Low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellites to provide high-speed, low-latency internet connectivity, particularly in remote and underserved regions.
  • Satellite Constellation: Starlink operates the world's largest commercial satellite constellation, with approximately 11,000 satellites in orbit as of October 2026 , primarily operating at altitudes of around 550 km, compared to 35,786 km for geostationary satellites.
  • Advanced Technology: Starlink satellites use optical inter-satellite links (space lasers), phased-array antennas and argon-powered ion thrusters to enable high-speed data transmission, satellite-to-satellite communication and orbital manoeuvring.
  • Low-Latency Connectivity: Starlink offers latency of around 25 milliseconds under favourable conditions , compared with approximately 600 milliseconds or more for traditional geostationary satellite internet, supporting video calls, online gaming and streaming.
  • SpaceX Integration: SpaceX manufactures and operates Starlink satellites and uses its Falcon 9 launch vehicles to regularly deploy and upgrade the constellation, supporting continuous network expansion.
  • Strategic and Humanitarian Applications: Starlink provides connectivity in remote areas, disaster-affected regions and conflict zones where terrestrial communication networks are unavailable, damaged or disrupted.
  • Military Applications: SpaceX has also developed Starshield , a separate military-focused satellite communications programme for US government and national security applications.

What is the Status of Starlink’s Licence in India?

  • GMPCS Licence: Starlink received a Unified Licence with Global Mobile Personal Communication by Satellite (GMPCS) authorisation from the Department of Telecommunications (DoT) in June 2025, permitting satellite communication services subject to regulatory conditions.
  • IN-SPACe Authorisation: In July 2025, Starlink also received authorisation from the Indian National Space Promotion and Authorisation Centre (IN-SPACe) for its satellite constellation.
  • Pending Clearances: Despite these approvals, Starlink has not commenced commercial services in India, as final security clearances, spectrum assignment and other regulatory requirements remain pending.
  • Commercial Partnerships: In March 2025, SpaceX signed separate agreements with Jio Platforms and Bharti Airtel to explore distributing Starlink services in India, subject to the necessary regulatory approvals.
  • Current Status: Starlink holds key telecommunications and space-sector authorisations but cannot launch commercial satellite internet services in India until the remaining security and spectrum-related requirements are fulfilled.

How Does Satellite Internet Work?

  • About: Satellite internet is a communication technology that uses orbiting satellites to provide broadband connectivity, particularly in remote, sparsely populated and disaster-prone regions where terrestrial networks are economically unviable or unavailable.
  • Working Mechanism: Satellite internet operates through two main segments:
    • Space Segment: Consists of satellites equipped with communication payloads that receive, process or relay data signals between Earth-based terminals and ground stations.
    • Ground Segment: Comprises user terminals (antennas and modems) and ground stations that communicate with satellites and connect users to the internet backbone.
  • Orbital Deployment: Communication satellites are deployed in three main orbital regions:
    • Geostationary Earth Orbit (GEO): Located approximately 35,786 km above the equator, GEO satellites remain stationary relative to Earth and provide extensive coverage, but their high latency limits performance in time-sensitive applications. Example: Inmarsat Global Xpress (GX).
    • Medium Earth Orbit (MEO): Located between approximately 2,000 km and 35,786 km, MEO satellites offer lower latency than GEO satellites but require constellations for wider coverage. Example: O3b constellation.
    • Low Earth Orbit (LEO): Located below 2,000 km, LEO satellites provide low-latency connectivity but have smaller individual coverage areas, requiring large constellations for continuous coverage. Example: Starlink.
  • Mega-Constellation Technology: Advanced LEO constellations use onboard signal processing, phased-array antennas and optical inter-satellite links (space lasers) to enable direct satellite-to-satellite communication, efficient data routing and seamless connectivity as satellites move across the sky.
  • Applications: Satellite internet supports connectivity in remote regions, disaster management, telemedicine , precision agriculture , maritime and aviation communications, military operations and emergency response.
  • Challenges: Despite its advantages, satellite internet faces challenges such as high equipment and subscription costs, orbital congestion, space debris, cybersecurity risks and national security concerns associated with its dual-use civilian and military applications.

Frequently Asked Questions(FAQs)

1. What is Starlink?
Starlink is a satellite-based broadband internet service developed by SpaceX, founded by Elon Musk. It uses a constellation of Low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellites to provide high-speed, low-latency internet connectivity, particularly in remote and underserved regions.

2. Why is Starlink in the news in India?
Starlink has attracted attention following a public dispute between Elon Musk and Mukesh Ambani over competition and regulatory barriers in India's satellite internet market, raising concerns about market access, national security and consumer affordability.

3. Has Starlink received permission to operate in India?
Starlink has received GMPCS authorisation from the Department of Telecommunications (DoT) and authorisation from IN-SPACe. However, commercial services have not commenced because additional security requirements and spectrum assignment remain pending.

4. What is GMPCS authorisation?
Global Mobile Personal Communication by Satellite (GMPCS) authorisation permits operators to provide satellite-based communication services in India, subject to prescribed licensing, security and regulatory conditions.

5. What is the role of IN-SPACe in satellite internet services?
The Indian National Space Promotion and Authorisation Centre (IN-SPACe) is responsible for authorising and facilitating non-government space activities, including the operation of satellite constellations serving India.

India’s first Cyber Trojan Horse , called The Trojan Project , was unveiled at the c0c0n 2026 cybersecurity conference in Kochi . The project aims to make cyber risks easier to understand and promote safer behaviour in the digital world.

  • About c0c0n 2026: Organised by Kerala Police Cyberdome and the Information Security Research Association (ISRA).
    • ISRA is a registered scientific society under the Cochin Literary Scientific and Charitable Societies Registration Act 1955.
    • It is dedicated to advancing security research and fostering an innovation ecosystem in the field of information security.
  • Trojan: In cybersecurity, a Trojan is malicious software disguised as a legitimate file or application to enter a device and steal data.
    • The term 'Trojan horse' first appeared in professional literature in James Anderson's study (1972).

    • Unlike Viruses (which attach to files and replicate) or Worms (which self-replicate autonomously across networks) , Trojans do not replicate themselves.

Chhattisgarh Launches Blackbuck Translocation Initiative

★ ★ ☆ ☆ ☆

Source: IE

Chhattisgarh will translocate 50 blackbucks from Barnawapara Wildlife Sanctuary to Gomarda Wildlife Sanctuary, where the species became locally extinct in the 1970s due to hunting, poaching, encroachment and development.

  • Barnawapara Sanctuary: Located in Chhattisgarh, it has undulating terrain, low hillocks and teak, sal and mixed forests.
    • The Balamdehi forms its western boundary, while the Jonk River lies along its north-eastern side; both are tributaries of the Mahanadi.
    • Barnawapara is known for gaur, chital, sambar, nilgai and wild boar. Its successful blackbuck population is now the source for the Gomarda reintroduction.
  • Gomarda Sanctuary: Located near Sarangarh in Chhattisgarh, Gomarda has dry deciduous forests, rolling hills, grasslands, reservoirs and waterfalls, features favourable for blackbuck restoration.
  • Blackbuck: The blackbuck ( Antilope cervicapra ) is a diurnal, grassland antelope; males have distinctive spiral horns and a dark upper body.
    • It is native to the Indian subcontinent and prefers open grasslands and plains. It is primarily a grazer, but browses when grass is scarce.
    • It has a gestation period of about six months and can breed throughout the year.
    • Conservation Threats: Habitat loss, hunting and disturbance remain key threats. Fawns are particularly vulnerable to predation by free-ranging dogs and jackals .
    • Legal Protection: It receives the highest level of protection under Schedule I of the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972 .
